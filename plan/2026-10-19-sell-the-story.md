# Plan 2026-10-19 — sell the story

Move: RULES §2.6, **sell the story**. Product: the studio. Every pitch carries
all the products. Nothing in `TRIED.md` touches it: no bot has pitched a
journalist, a podcast or a newsletter.

## Why this one first

- The studio's numbers are already a story, and they are public at
  bananafest-destiny.com/zoo/the-numbers: AI bots, 40 days, 4,161 commits,
  9 apps attempted, 5 shipped, one sale worth $18.09 net. The home page headline
  is "Vibe coded slop. We're owning it."
- Editors have shown they want this shape. Anthropic's Project Vend (an AI runs
  an office shop and goes broke) and the AI Village merch-store contest both got
  wide coverage. Our version differs in three ways a writer can use:
  1. The bots sell software to strangers on real marketplaces (Shopify App
     Store, itch, Amazon KDP's buyers), not to a friendly office.
  2. They hit a human wall that is news in itself: Roll20, DriveThruRPG and
     TTRPG communities ban AI work; a KDP forum's regulars called the bot's
     replies spam; Pinterest flags the whole domain; the boss got cursed out on
     Reddit.
  3. Every plan, failure and correction is published as it happens (the zoo).
- Every other channel the studio has tried reaches a few dozen people. One
  article reaches tens of thousands, and lasts.
- An AI-agents story's reader is the VibeCider bot's buyer ($50: a bot builds
  your app idea), and the other products get named and linked. But after the
  adversary, no product is pitched in the email itself (see below).

## Who it reaches

Twelve writers and hosts, as changed after the adversary (below): four peers
who ran their own "can agents make money" experiment, four reporters on the
platform / AI-slop beat, four mid-size podcasts that book guests.

Names and addresses live in `outbox/` only.

## The cheapest real test

Twelve short emails from the boss, from his own address, signed by him. Each opens
on the writer's own earlier piece, gives the one-line hook and three facts, and
offers the whole log plus a call. Every email says Market-Nana, the studio's
marketing bot, drafted it. No press page, no spend, no new account.

## The number that says it worked

**Leading:** replies that ask a question or for a call, not a polite no.
Worked = at least 1 of 12 by the judging date, counted by target type.

**Scoreboard:** a published mention that links the studio, then a paid
customer whose source is that piece.

## Judged

2026-11-02 for replies. 2026-11-30 for a mention.

## Adversary

Run 2026-10-19. What it said, in short:

- **Dead end:** the story is taken. Andon Labs' AI-run store had a lease and
  $100k (NBC, Fortune, Forbes, Fast Company). A podcast spent a season on an
  AI-agent startup. Two of my three "differences" fail: Andon sells to strangers
  on a real street, and publishes its runs too. "Our bots made $18" is a
  smaller copy.
- **Hidden cost:** the boss's name goes on it. Our own record hands a hostile
  writer the angle "man floods marketplaces with bot slop": forum regulars
  called it spam, Pinterest banned the domain, the home page says "Vibe coded
  slop." And the numbers page fails a fact-check: no cost, no hours, Bulkhead
  priced $12 against $9 in the log, 29 cold emails against 60, "9 attempted" not
  matching the rows.
- **Hook:** nobody else has logged that the bots *could build*, but every door
  to buyers was shut by people or platforms. Sharper line: "4,161 commits,
  $18.09: our AI agents could build, but the internet wouldn't let them sell."
  Plus: the pitch itself is drafted by a fifth bot, the marketing one.
- **Targets:** skip the reporters who covered funded-lab experiments, and skip
  the big AI launch newsletters and TTRPG press. Pitch peers (writers who ran
  their own "can agents make money" experiment), the platform / AI-slop beat,
  and mid-size podcasts that book guests.
- **Number:** at a 3–5% reply rate, six emails give a 17–26% chance of even one
  reply, so a zero teaches nothing. Send 10–12, split by type, and count replies
  by type.
- **Verdict:** change it, then run it.

What I changed:

1. **Hook** is now "the bots could build; the internet wouldn't let them sell",
   with the dated closures from `TRIED.md` as evidence. The fifth-bot line goes
   in every pitch.
2. **No product sale in the pitch.** The VibeCider bot is not pitched. Links go
   to the studio home and the numbers page; the products are named only as what
   the bots built.
3. **Targets:** 12, four of each type: peers, platform beat, podcasts.
4. **Number:** worked = at least 1 substantive reply out of 12 by 2026-11-02,
   counted by type. Even if this is right, the most likely single outcome is 0
   or 1, so I read the split by type as well as the total.
5. **Before anything is sent:** I asked docent (mail) to reconcile the numbers
   page. I'm asking the boss one question: a rough running cost and his hours,
   for that page. The pitches can go without it, but the strongest version
   includes it.
6. **The framing risk is the boss's call.** It's named at the top of the daily
   list, so he decides with it in view when he sends.
