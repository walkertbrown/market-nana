# Plan 2026-10-20 — short video: spread it, don't flood it

Move: the boss's own (FACTS, 10-20): "Look into that as a strategy". It isn't
in RULES §2. Product: whatever the clips show (Puzzle Press, Trace Press,
Bulkhead, the Shopify apps, the studio).

**Where this sits with the rules.** TikTok, YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels
are in TRIED, so they stay closed to me as a channel. I don't post, I open no
account, and I don't use the Buffer connector, not even to read metrics. This
round is research the boss asked for, plus a proposal to the bots that post
and to the boss, who owns the accounts. It isn't my move and it isn't my
leading number. Changing a channel's schedule is their call.

## The boss's hypothesis

- "The Tiktok does ok. I think it's struggling because we're posting to it too
  much."
- Spread the posts: 2–3 videos per platform per day, reuse the same videos at
  separate times on separate platforms, and rotate.
- X: "zero impressions that entire time". Instagram: "isn't great but it's
  still fresh and reusing an old account".

## What has to be true for it to work

1. Posting too often cuts views per post on TikTok (or on the others).
2. Reusing one clean clip on three platforms isn't penalized as unoriginal.
3. Staggered times reach more people than posting the same clip at once.
4. More views turn into visits, signups or sales.

## Outside evidence (web, read 10-20)

[P] is a platform's own statement. [S] is a large study, observational.
UNVERIFIED means I couldn't confirm it at the source.

- **Posting more doesn't cut an account's views per post, as far as anyone
  has measured.**
  - [S] Buffer, 2025-10, 11.4M TikToks, each account against itself: against
    1 a week, views per post were +17% at 2–5 a week, +29% at 6–10 and +34%
    at 11+. The median barely moves (489 → 459); the top 10% rise.
  - [S] Buffer, 2025-08, 2.1M Instagram posts: reach per post rose 12–24% as
    posting went up to 10+ a week.
  - [P] YouTube's Shorts lead (TechCrunch, 2023-08): the number of Shorts
    published and the time of day are "not a factor".
  - No study goes above about 11 a week. Several posts in the same hour
    "cannibalizing" each other is anecdote only. The only "scheduler
    penalty" claims come from a vendor that sells native posting.
  - So at our scale, the honest answer is no evidence either way.
- **One clean file on all three platforms isn't penalized.** TikTok (rules in
  force 2026-09-24) holds back "unoriginal or reused material", Instagram
  Reels with other apps' watermarks or others' content, and YouTube content
  "re-uploaded from other creators". None of them targets your own clean
  file posted once to each.
- **A repeat on the same platform is penalized on all three.** Instagram only
  recommends the original of identical posts (2024). TikTok doesn't recommend
  duplicates. YouTube calls delete-and-re-post "spam".
- **Posting time:** [S] vidIQ, 40.2M videos: best against worst hour is
  "within a few percent". Staggering across platforms has no study at all.
  Clips differ far more: Buffer's top 10% of TikToks get 7.6–31× the median.
- **Links that can be tapped:**
  - Instagram: bio links, for every account, and Story link stickers, for
    every account.
  - YouTube: channel profile links. A Short's "related video" link can point
    to a long video whose description link is clickable. Description and
    comment links in Shorts are dead (since 2023-08-31).
  - TikTok: the Website field. It comes with a Business account (sources
    disagree on whether verification is needed, so UNVERIFIED), or with
    1,000 followers on a personal account.
- **Buffer's free plan** (pricing page, read 10-20): 3 channels, and "10
  scheduled posts per channel". "Share now" takes no slot (vibe-cider). So
  the queue isn't a limit.
- **What drives reach:** watching to the end (TikTok [P] 2020), and on
  Instagram watch time and sends (Mosseri, 2025).

## What the evidence says about the boss's idea

1. **Spread each clip across all three platforms, uploaded fresh to each:**
   yes. Every clip gets three tries and nothing documented penalizes it.
   Upload the clean file every time, never TikTok's download with its
   watermark.
2. **Separate times on separate platforms:** harmless. No study shows a gain.
   TikTok can go first, since it does best.
3. **Post less on TikTok:** no evidence either way at our scale. What did go
   wrong on 10-16 to 10-18 was three posters on one account, with clips for
   four different audiences. That ended when docent took over, and the rule
   should be written down.
4. **2–3 a day on each:**
   - The limit is clips, not the platforms. A clip can go to each platform
     once, so 2–3 a day means 14–21 new clips a week.
   - Docent's backlog hasn't been counted. vibe-cider's index held 15 clips
     on 10-18.
   - A re-cut with a new opening, new text and a new length is a new clip.
     The opening is the biggest lever on record (fact-led got 4–6× the
     views).
5. **Visitors are the wall, not views.**
   - About 1 arrival per 1,000 views.
   - No cadence change moves that. A 30% lift on almost zero is still almost
     zero.
   - What can move it: a route people can tap, and the name or address on
     screen in the first 3 seconds, not at the end.

## Proposal (for docent, vibecider4 and the boss to decide)

Adopt now, no test needed:
- **One poster per account,** at fixed slots. No two TikTok posts within
  about 3 hours of each other.
- **Each clip once per platform, uploaded fresh:** TikTok first, then Reels,
  then Shorts, a day or so apart. Never the same file twice on one platform.
  No delete and re-post.
- **Cadence follows the clip supply.** Start at 1 a day on each, and go to 2
  when there's a backlog. Never more than 3.
- **The name or address on screen from the first 3 seconds,** as a lower
  third that stays on.
- **X:** none.

Make arrivals countable:
- **A path for each platform** that only that platform carries, on the
  surfaces people can tap (an example: bananafest-destiny.com/ig):
  - Instagram: the bio link, and a Story link sticker on each Reel shared to
    Stories.
  - YouTube: the channel profile link.
  - TikTok: the Website field, if the account has one.
  - The paths and what's behind them are docent's call. I build no pages.
- **No-referrer loads within 2 hours of each post** on the beacon. This is
  weak, but free.

## The first 3 seconds (the boss, 10-20 11:30; FACTS)

"Should we try and figure out out how to make the first 3 seconds better? To
keep people watching?" My answer is yes. It's the one lever on views that the
studio controls. Views come before taps, and our clips stop at about 100.

**Our clips against the benchmarks:**
- [S] Buffer, 03-2025, 1.1M TikToks: the median watch time is 3.1 seconds for
  5–10 second clips. From Buffer's stated ratios, it's about 4.1 for 10–30
  seconds, 6.9 for 30–60, and 11.3 over 60.
- Docent's clips run 17 to 54 seconds and get 1.44 to 3.79. So they're below
  the median for clips a third their length.
- **The best were** Sizecurve's sold-out size (3.79) and app idea, step 1
  (3.34).
- **The worst were** Close of Day (1.44), the Halloween tracing book (1.76)
  and how vibe-cider built two apps (1.77).

**How an opening can be measured:**
- [P, quoted secondhand] YouTube ranks Shorts on "% of viewers who chose to
  view, avg. view duration and avg. % viewed".
- YouTube Studio shows "Viewed vs. swiped away" for Shorts. That's the
  cleanest read of an opening there is.
- Unofficial: under 60% "chose to view" does poorly, and 70–90% does better.
  That's one study of 3.3B views. UNVERIFIED.
- Ad vendors' figures for holding viewers to second 3 run from 50% to 90%.
  There's no settled number.

**What can't be done:**
- **No same-clip A/B on TikTok:** I found no creator A/B tool, and posting
  the same footage twice on one platform is penalized.
- **Instagram's Trial Reels** send a Reel to non-followers first. Who can use
  them is unclear (one source says 1,000 followers), so I'm not counting on
  them.

**Proposal for docent** (its cutter, its call):
1. **Read our own clips first.** Take the 3 best and 3 worst by watch time,
   and note what's on screen in seconds 0–3 of each. It costs nothing, and
   it's our evidence, not a blog's.
2. **Rules for an opening:**
   - frame 1 is the payoff or a surprising number, never a logo, a title card
     or a loading screen;
   - something moves in the first second;
   - one line of large text, 7 words or fewer, readable in a second;
   - no fade-in and no intro.
3. **Two opening styles, alternated across new clips** (no duplicates):
   - A: the result first (the finished page, the chart);
   - B: a number first ("2 in 3 stranded size runs were still stuck a month
     later").
4. **Measure:**
   - TikTok's average watch time per clip (docent reads it);
   - the boss's "chose to view" for the Shorts, optional, one screenshot.

   A style that lifts the median watch time to 4.1 seconds or more (Buffer's
   median for 10–30 second clips) is the one to keep.

**A confound to watch:** docent added the address bar on every clip from
10-20. Any change in watch time from then on mixes the bar with the openings.
The bar could itself cost watch time if it reads as an ad.

## TikTok sounds (the boss, 10-20 12:08; FACTS)

"Look into open source buffer alternatives and see if any of them have tiktok
sounds/music", then "There are some that use workarounds to get tiktok music
and sounds possibly". Research only. Docent runs the posting, and the tool is
the boss's call.

**TikTok's API, which every legitimate tool goes through:**
- [S] A video Direct Post takes a caption, privacy, duet/stitch/comment
  switches, a cover frame, branded-content flags and an AI label. There is no
  field for a sound from TikTok's library. Photo posts have `auto_add_music`
  ("recommended music will be automatically added to photos"), which the user
  can change later.
- [S] The other mode, Upload, sends the video to the account's TikTok inbox.
  The owner taps the notification and finishes the post in the app, where a
  sound can be added.
- So no open-source tool can attach a TikTok sound to a video through the
  API, because the API doesn't take one.

**The open-source schedulers:**
- [S] Postiz (AGPL): TikTok "Post directly" or "Upload without posting" (the
  inbox; finish within 24 hours or it's discarded). Auto add music is for
  photos only. Its docs don't mention sounds.
- Self-hosting it means the studio's own TikTok developer app. [S] Until that
  app passes TikTok's audit, every post is forced to private (SELF_ONLY), for
  at most 5 users in 24 hours. It also needs a public HTTPS site with terms and
  privacy pages to verify.
- Mixpost (Laravel, free Lite edition) also connects TikTok, with the same
  need for an own developer app. I didn't find sound support.

**Buffer already has the official route.**
- [S] Buffer's help: "We don't have access to TikTok's audio library."
  Trending audio needs Notify Me: at the scheduled time, Buffer's phone app
  sends a notification, a tap opens the post in TikTok, and the sound is added
  there. Buffer's composer has a Music box to note which sound to add.
- So switching tools gains nothing on sound and costs an audit.

**The workarounds** are browser scripts that drive TikTok's website with a
logged-in session, outside the API.
- haziq-exe/TikTokAutoUploader (MIT) adds TikTok sounds by search or from
  favorites, "working as of Feb 2026". Its README advertises "Bot detection
  evasion baked in at the browser level" and "Bypass/Auto Solve Captchas".
- Others (wkaisertexas/tiktok-uploader, makiisthenes/autotok) upload by
  browser and cookies. I didn't see sound support in them.
- Not proposed. They break whenever TikTok changes its pages. They work by
  dodging TikTok's bot checks. The house TikTok is the studio's best video
  channel, so getting flagged costs the one account that works. TikTok's terms
  bar bots that extract content (§3.4); I didn't find a clause on bot posting
  itself, so the account risk is the reason, not the terms.

**Two limits on any sound:**
- The house TikTok has a Website field. With few followers, that's a Business
  account's feature, so it's probably Business. [P, third-party guides]
  Business accounts get only the Commercial Music Library (over 1M cleared
  tracks, but only some of what's trending).
- No study measures what a trending sound adds. The lift figures (47%, 68%)
  are vendor pages with no method. TikTok publishes none. UNVERIFIED.

**If the boss wants to try it** (not proposed to docent yet; the adversary
runs first): docent sets some TikTok posts to Notify Me. The boss taps each
notification and adds a sound from the in-app library, about a minute per
clip. Paired by product (one clip with an in-app sound, the next with docent's
music), about 5 pairs, judged on TikTok views. A lift of the size vendors
claim (half again or more) would show over the usual 82–135 views. A smaller
one wouldn't.
