# Round 14: links to the bar app's free inventory sheet (cider2's ask, 10-26)

**Why:** The boss asked cider2 for ways to get links to the bar app's site, and
named me. Search Console, 3 months: 449 impressions, 0 clicks, position 31.
280 of those impressions are the free liquor inventory spreadsheet page, at
22 to 41 for "liquor inventory spreadsheet" and similar. The page targets
the right words; the site has no links. (RULES §2.4: write to the people who
publish the lists. I build no pages.)

**What the search found (11:00 to 11:25):**
- **The free bar sheet is the most crowded free thing in the trade.** Toast,
  Smartsheet, BinWise, Backbar, BevSpot, WISK, Provi, Sculpture, MarketMan,
  Jotform, Spreadsheet Daddy and Fit Small Business all give one away. Every
  vendor page links its own. Nobody links a rival's.
- **The "best bar inventory software" lists** are SourceForge, Slashdot,
  Gitnux and Guideflow: directories or paid placement. Closed.
- **Curated newsletters:** one bar-owner Substack shows no contact route. One
  hospitality newsletter that links outside tools has been silent since
  2025-10-21. Neither is worth a send.
- **A famous bartender's free calculators page** lists only his own.
- **The weighing-vs-tenths page** is explanation, with no measurement of its
  own, so it isn't a reason to link either.
- **What's left: people who teach beverage cost.** A classroom has no product
  to protect, and a free sheet with no sign-up is a handout. Community-college
  courses on bar inventory and beverage cost controls are listed below.

**The move:** the boss emails 4 instructors of beverage cost courses, offering
the sheet and the tenths chart as a free classroom handout.
- Who: hospitality instructors at four US community colleges whose course
  outlines name liquor inventory or beverage cost control.
- Cheapest test: 4 emails from the boss.
- Number: pass if one links it from a course page or asks for it to use in
  class; 0 replies is a stop.
- Judging date: 11-16.

**Adversary:** instructors teach from textbooks, and a link may sit on a
login-only course page (Canvas, Blackboard) that Google never sees. Then the
link is worth nothing for search, but a class of future bar managers still
sees it. The real fix for links is something only shelfcall has, because a
sheet is a commodity. That's cider2's to build, if it wants. A first measurement
of its own, for example how far eyeballed tenths fall from a scale on a set
of real bottles, would give a trade writer a reason to cite it.
